# Response to Reviewer 1 Comment 3

We thank the reviewer for highlighting the need to evaluate explanation stability and the influence of the reference spectrum. We have quantified animal-level variability in the existing out-of-fold zone explanations and performed an additional controlled reference-sensitivity analysis.

For each held-out animal, we calculated mean absolute zone contributions over its ten explained spectra and summarized ranks, top-three frequency and rank concordance across the twelve animals. In the raw full-fingerprint logistic regression model, Amide I, Amide II and PO4 were in the top three for 11/12, 10/12 and 10/12 animals, respectively. The corresponding XGBoost frequencies were 12/12, 11/12 and 10/12. Median pairwise animal-rank correlations were 0.855 for logistic regression and 0.885 for XGBoost. Thus, the pooled leading regions are frequent across animals, while the added tables also document exceptions rather than implying identical rankings in every specimen.

For the additional sensitivity analysis, each fold model and the same ten held-out spectra were kept fixed while the training-mean reference was replaced by the coordinate-wise training median or three empirical background panels. Each panel contained one complete spectrum from each of the eleven training animals. Coalition output probabilities were averaged over panel spectra. All references were constructed exclusively from training animals, with no held-out animal used for scaling, fitting or reference construction.

Amide I, Amide II and PO4 remained globally leading for both models under every tested reference. Median within-animal rank correlations for mean versus median were 0.988 for logistic regression and 0.976 for XGBoost. Across empirical panels, median correlations ranged from 0.945–0.976 and 0.939–0.964, respectively. Expanding the training-mean explanations from ten to fifty spectra per animal also retained the same global leading regions. Attribution magnitudes and some individual-animal top-three sets changed, so we limit the conclusion to robustness of the broad regional interpretation.

We clarify that replacing a zone with a reference measures its contribution relative to that reference; it does not physically remove the biochemical constituent. A mean or median spectrum need not represent an observed specimen, and replacing whole zones can still disrupt dependencies between zones and create unrealistic hybrid spectra. The empirical-background analysis examines a limited alternative and does not establish conditional or causal attributions. Exact coalition enumeration ensures exact Shapley values for the specified game, but does not remove uncertainty in the masking assumptions.

The added Methods, Results, animal-level tables and sensitivity figure report these procedures and limitations. Because each LOGO fold corresponds to one animal and the training sets overlap, the stability summaries are descriptive and the biological sample size remains twelve.
